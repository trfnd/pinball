The real lesson of Part 2

Reliability is designed

A better model doesn't make the work reliable. What makes it reliable is how the work is set up around the model: who does which part, what gets checked, against what, and who signs off. That has to be designed before the work starts — not bolted on after something goes wrong.

Six shapes agents get put together in

You've now built two of these yourself. There's a small, reusable family — each one fits a different job. The skill isn't knowing a model; it's choosing the shape.

1 · Classify-and-act

Sort each item into a bucket, then take the matching action. Triage, routing, closing the books. You met this in Module 06.

2 · Fan-out-and-synthesize · Built · M07

Split into parallel specialists, then one reconciles them. Research, a go/no-go, anything with genuinely distinct parts.

3 · Adversarial verification · Built · M09

Independent reviewers, blind to each other, try to prove the work wrong before you trust it. High-stakes checks.

4 · Generate-and-filter

Produce many candidates, then filter hard to the few good ones. Naming, designs, a wall of options.

5 · Tournament

Compare candidates head-to-head in rounds until one wins. Picking the single best of many.

6 · Loop-until-done

Repeat until a stated condition is met. Cleanups, "keep going until every test passes."

How do you decide which to use? Match the shape to the job — one item to route (1), a big question with distinct parts (2), a result you can't afford to get wrong (3), a space to explore (4), one best pick (5), or a target to grind toward (6). None of it is the model. It's the design around it.

Checkpoint

Could you hand the verified recap to someone to run without you — and name the one check that would catch the mistake before it reached them?

Make the check real

Ask for the single verification that would have caught Module 06's errors:

What's the ONE check that, if it ran every time, would have caught the $32 Saturday and the 45-lemon day on its own? Write it as a rule the verifier runs before any recap is released.

A check you can state in one sentence — and that would have caught the last real mistake — is worth more than a verifier that "looks it over."

A recap you'd trust unwatched — because the check is designed in, not hoped for.

Reliability is designed. The verified recap isn't more trustworthy because the model got smarter — it's trustworthy because you put an independent check between the work and the world, and decided who signs off.

And when it does go wrong one day: someone answers for it. Part of designing the system is naming who — before you need to.
