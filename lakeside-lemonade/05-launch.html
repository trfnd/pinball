This is where the work stops being notes and becomes something real. The first draft comes back fast and fine — and generic. The interesting part is what you ask it to change, and which of your earlier decisions quietly dominate the page while others barely show up at all.

1Get it standing

Bring in everything from Part 1 and ask for the simplest possible version — a site that exists:

Build a one-page website for my lemonade stand using everything we decided: the customer, the corner, the price, and the brand.

It comes back fast, and it works. It's also a clean default template that could belong to any stand in any town — a generic header, a stock photo, your decisions flattened into placeholder copy. That's fine. You needed something standing before you could make it yours.

2Make it the version you'd share

Now push it from fine to yours. Name the changes you want, and ask to see what moved:

Good start. Now make three changes: give it a look that matches my brand's voice (not a default template), lead with the real story — a small family stand on the waterfront — and cut anything that's taking over the page for no reason. Show me a before/after of what you changed.

Now it reads like your stand — the waterfront story up top, your brand's voice in the copy, the filler gone. And the before/after is the useful part: it shows you which of your Part 1 decisions actually made it onto the page, and which ones you'd assumed were there but weren't.

The shape underneath

The five-module pattern

Customer → location → price → brand → launch isn't just this course. It's a reusable shape for almost any small venture: figure out who it's for, where it lives, what it costs, how it sounds, and how people see it. You just ran it end to end with AI.

Checkpoint

Does the finished site reflect the decisions you actually made — or did some earlier output quietly take over the page?

See what the model weighted

Paste this into the same chat and read the answer carefully:

Which of my earlier decisions show up most on this page, and which barely show up at all? If anything is prominent that I didn't emphasize, tell me why it's there.

What shows up most is a clue to what the model weighted — sometimes it's not what you'd have chosen.
