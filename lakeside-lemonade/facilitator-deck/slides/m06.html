Where your instructions stop, the agent decides.

Every gap is a judgment call: a visit one day out of window, an ambiguous unit, two documents that disagree.

Build in pauses: ask for the plan before it acts, and name the calls it must bring back to you.

Ask for the log: an explanation afterwards is a story. A log says which file changed, from what to what, and when.

And still: "You remain responsible for all actions taken by Claude performed on your behalf."

Which decisions should never be made silently?

List two judgment calls an agent might quietly make in your work. For each: stop and ask, flag and continue, or never delegate?

Anthropic: Building effective agents · Use Claude Cowork safely · Economic Index

In the module, the missing starting cash was a gap the agent had to fill. In research work, gaps look like a visit one day outside its window, an ambiguous unit, or two documents that disagree. Every gap becomes a judgment call made on your behalf. Anthropic's agent guidance answers with structure. Agents "pause for human feedback at checkpoints or when encountering blockers," with "stopping conditions (such as a maximum number of iterations) to maintain control." One of its core principles is transparency: "explicitly showing the agent's planning steps." So ask for the plan before it acts, and name the calls it must bring back to you. Clinical and scientific judgments — adverse-event causality, classifying a deviation — stay with qualified people. On records: your teams already value a record made at the time. An agent's explanation afterwards is a story; a log is evidence. Anthropic's Cowork safety guidance: "watch for unexpected patterns: Is Claude accessing files or websites you didn't mention? Is the task scope creeping beyond what you asked for? If something feels off, stop the task immediately," and "You remain responsible for all actions taken by Claude performed on your behalf." Example (ours): an agent compares a de-identified listing with the schedule of assessments in an approved environment and outputs flags plus a change log, changing nothing in the source; data management confirms every flag. Why it matters more every month: in Anthropic's Economic Index, "directive" conversations (a whole task handed over) rose from 27% of sampled Claude.ai conversations in late 2024 to 39% in August 2025, then 32% in November 2025; Anthropic still reads the trend as toward more automation. Discussion: groups, 4 minutes. Typical answers: borderline eligibility, deviation classification, clinical significance, which document version is current. Push on the second question.
