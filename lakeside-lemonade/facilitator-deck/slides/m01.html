01 · Customer · Description

Brief Claude like a brilliant colleague on their first day.

Anthropic's own advice: "Think of Claude as a brilliant but new employee who lacks context on your norms and workflows."

The colleague test: if someone with minimal context would be confused by your prompt, Claude will be too.

Describe three things: the product you want back, the process to follow, and how it should work with you.

The part people skip: people said how Claude should interact in only 30% of conversations studied. Try "Push back if my assumptions are wrong."

Discuss · pairs · 4 min

What does a new colleague get on day one that Claude never does?

List three things you'd hand them before they draft anything. Which could you paste into a chat? Which live only in your head?

Lakeside Lemonade · Debrief

Anthropic prompting guidance · AI Fluency (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) · AI Fluency Index

Move from the stand to your own team. Think about the last time someone new joined to help write a protocol or a report. You didn't say "write section 8". You gave them the template, the last approved version, the reviewer comments, and you told them who reads it. Claude gets none of that unless you provide it. Anthropic's prompting guide: "Think of Claude as a brilliant but new employee who lacks context on your norms and workflows. The more precisely you explain what you want, the better the result." Its golden rule: show your prompt to a colleague with minimal context; if they'd be confused, Claude will be too. The AI Fluency course splits Description into three parts. Product: what you want, including format, audience and style. Process: how to approach it, such as "first list every visit in the schedule of activities, then check each against the body text." Performance: how to work with you, such as "flag anything you couldn't locate rather than guessing." Performance is the part people skip: Anthropic's AI Fluency Index (9,830 conversations, one week in January 2026) found users said how Claude should interact in only 30% of them. Correlational; early-adopter sample. Discussion: pairs for 4 minutes, then two or three answers. Listen for the template, the last approved version, committee comments, who reads it, and the unwritten things (what the sponsor always pushes back on, which sites struggle). Write the "only in my head" items on the flip chart; they lead into Module 02.
