Two moves from the module, zoomed out. 1. Questions before drafting. Anthropic's clearest wording is in its Claude Code docs, written for software work: "For larger features, have Claude interview you first," with a sample prompt that says "Don't ask obvious questions, dig into the hard parts I might not have considered." Adapt it to an SOP: "Before drafting, ask me the questions you need about scope, roles, deviations and records." Anthropic's balance: questions are worth it when different readings "would lead to materially different work," and you should still give the task, intent and constraints up front. 2. Citations are receipts, not reasons. A sample-size assumption taken from a published trial can be cited correctly and still be wrong for your population. Anthropic's "Reduce hallucinations" page lists the techniques: allow "I don't know"; for long documents, extract word-for-word quotes first; verify with citations and retract any claim without a supporting quote; restrict to the documents supplied. Its limit: "while these techniques significantly reduce hallucinations, they don't eliminate them entirely. Always validate critical information, especially for high-stakes decisions." The evidence: in Anthropic's AI Fluency Index (9,830 conversations, January 2026), conversations that produced an artifact had better briefs (+14.7 points clarifying the goal) but weaker checking: less likely to spot missing context (−5.2), check facts (−3.7) or question the reasoning (−3.1). One week, early adopters, correlation not cause. Discussion: pairs, 4 minutes. Trace one real number's check: who, against which source, where it's recorded.
