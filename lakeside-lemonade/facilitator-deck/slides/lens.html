The lens for the whole day

AI fluency comes down to four Ds.

Delegation

Setting goals and deciding whether, when and how to engage with AI

Description

Effectively describing goals to prompt useful AI behaviors and outputs

Discernment

Accurately assessing the usefulness of AI outputs and behaviors

Diligence

Taking responsibility for what we do with AI and how we do it

Ground rules for trying this at work: approved tools only · no identifiable personal data or confidential documents in tools not approved for them · a named person signs off.

Lakeside Lemonade · Debrief

Adapted from AI Fluency (Dakan, Feller and Anthropic) · CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

Set the rhythm first: each module takes about 20 minutes on laptops; each debrief here is 5 to 10 minutes. Then introduce the lens that holds the deck together. The four Ds come from Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller and are taught in Anthropic's free AI Fluency course, which defines fluency as "interacting with AI systems in ways that are effective, efficient, ethical and safe." The definitions on the slide are the course's own words. Every debrief slide names the D it worked in its top label. Also mention the three ways of working: automation (AI carries out a specific task you specify), augmentation (you think together, back and forth) and agency (you set AI up to do future tasks on its own). Modules 01 to 05 are mostly augmentation; 06 to 10 move toward agency. The ground rules are ours, not Anthropic's, and they matter more than any prompt tip. Nothing today replaces your SOPs, ethics and regulatory requirements. Ask: "Which D feels strongest for you today, and which weakest?" Ask also: "What is one document you'd love to spend less time on?" Write those on a flip chart; we return to it in the finale.
