05 · Launch · Description + Discernment

Describe, discern, refine, and then you decide.

The loop: say what you want, judge what comes back, say what worked and adjust. Then you "make the final decisions about what to keep, modify, or discard."

Iterating pays: people who iterated were 5.6× more likely to question Claude's reasoning. Experienced users iterate more, and succeed more often.

In practice: build long documents section by section, not in one big request.

AI Fluency course (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) · AI Fluency Index · Anthropic Economic Index

The AI Fluency course turns iteration into four steps: describe, discern, refine, then bring in your expertise, which includes "Make the final decisions about what to keep, modify, or discard" and "Take responsibility for the final output." Anthropic's prompting guide calls the same shape its "most common chaining pattern": generate a draft, review it against criteria, refine. Its own example uses a medical paper: summarize the methodology, findings and clinical implications, grade the summary, then improve it. Evidence from two Anthropic datasets. AI Fluency Index: 85.7% of sampled conversations iterated; those showed 2.67 extra fluency behaviors versus 1.33, and people who iterated were 5.6 times more likely to question Claude's reasoning and 4 times more likely to spot missing context. Economic Index: users with six or more months of experience iterate more, and their conversations succeed about 3 to 5 percentage points more often. All observational; Claude judged success. Discussion: tables, 5 minutes. Map the five steps onto a real study or major document. Customer: who reads it or lives with it (reviewers, sites, participants)? Location: where is it used, in which systems? Price: staff time and participant burden. Brand: plain language, house style, regulatory voice. Launch: what goes out, to whom, after whose sign-off. Expect Price to surface participant burden; note that Launch already includes sign-off — Module 10 arriving early.
