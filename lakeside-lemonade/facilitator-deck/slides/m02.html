In the module, a local tip changed Claude's answer. Research work is full of that kind of tacit knowledge: which visits sites find hard to schedule, what the review committee always asks, why a window is plus or minus three days and not five. Claude can't know any of it. Anthropic's Help Center: "Think of Claude as a newly-hired contractor. It doesn't have any context about you, your task, or your organization." The AI Fluency course makes the same point from the human side: "Effective delegation requires both domain expertise and an understanding of AI capabilities." Your expertise isn't replaced; it is the input. Why not just paste everything? Chroma's 2025 "Context Rot" report tested 18 models and found performance "grows increasingly unreliable as input length grows." Anthropic's context-engineering post says models have an "attention budget" and recommends "the smallest possible set of high-signal tokens." Two balancing points: it's "a performance gradient rather than a hard cliff," and "minimal does not necessarily mean short." Practical habits: paste the sections that matter, not the whole 150-page document plus amendments; label each document with name and version; keep a short decisions-and-open-issues note; when a chat gets long, start fresh from a summary and re-check that critical details survived. The payoff is the second question: writing tacit knowledge down for Claude often helps the next human colleague too, and it is raw material for a Skill (Module 08).
