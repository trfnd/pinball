Lakeside Lemonade · Debrief

Anthropic: Claude for Life Sciences · Doshi and Hauser, Science Advances 2024

Step back from the brand kit. A Skill is a packaged method, and Anthropic promotes Skills to life-science teams for exactly this: "Skills are a natural fit for scientific work, since they allow Claude to consistently and predictably follow specific protocols and procedures." In January 2026 Anthropic added a sample skill for generating clinical trial protocol drafts; it produces drafts that need expert review, not a finished tool. The evidence. Doshi and Hauser (Science Advances, 2024) ran a controlled experiment: 293 writers wrote short stories with no AI, one AI idea, or up to five; 600 evaluators made 3,519 ratings. With up to five ideas, stories were rated 8.1% more novel and 9.0% more useful, and the least creative writers gained the most. But the stories became more similar to each other and to the AI's ideas. Writers rating their own stories saw no difference. The authors call it a social dilemma: "writers are individually better off, but collectively a narrower scope of novel content is produced." Limits: a short creative task, no interactive chat, nothing tested on scientific writing — treat it as a caution for brainstorming. Discussion: groups, 4 minutes. Collect two lists on the flip chart. "Same every time": headings, required consent elements, terminology, table formats. "Never generic": rationale, design choices, the risk discussion, the research question. The practice on the slide (alone first, then deliberately different alternatives, then merge, tracking which ideas came from people) reduces fixation on the first suggestion. Close with the module's point: the kit gets everyone to "good"; your corrections make it yours.
