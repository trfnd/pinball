Part 2 · Agents and systems

From answers to actions.

The question changes from "Is this right?" to "What did it decide, and what did it touch?"

Workflow

The model and tools follow steps you set in advance. Predictable.

Agent

The model directs its own process and chooses its tools: "LLMs autonomously using tools in a loop." Flexible.

Anthropic: Building effective agents · Effective context engineering · AI Fluency (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)

Reset the room for Part 2. Part 1 was a conversation: Claude suggested and you decided. From here, Claude acts. It opens files, changes them, and takes steps you didn't spell out. In the AI Fluency framework this is the move from augmentation toward agency, where "The human establishes the AI's knowledge and behavior patterns rather than specifying exact actions." All four Ds still apply, but Diligence comes first. Anthropic separates two kinds of agentic systems. Workflows "are systems where LLMs and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths." Agents "dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage"; in 2025 Anthropic simplified this to "LLMs autonomously using tools in a loop." A research example of a workflow: "every Monday, turn this enrollment export into the same three tables." An agent: "go through this folder of draft documents, sort, summarize and flag what needs me," choosing its own steps. Anthropic's advice is conservative: "For many applications, however, optimizing single LLM calls with retrieval and in-context examples is usually enough." Choose for the task, not the novelty. Ask: "Which of your recurring tasks follows a fixed path, and which really needs judgment along the way?"
