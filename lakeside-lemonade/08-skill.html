Module 08 · Part 2 — Agents & systems

Can you bottle what you know?

Scenario
There's a thing you do the same way every time — say, your weekly stand recap. You're going to package it into a Skill so anyone, or an agent, can run it the way you would.
Your role
You supply the expertise. The Skill only carries what you manage to put in it.
What you'll practice
Writing a Skill from your own method — and finding exactly where your judgment refuses to fit in the box.
Tool
Claude, with a skill-creator.

You don't need a file. Describe the task to Claude in your own words — what you do, in what order, and why. Then bring one example you can grade it against: a case where you already know the right answer.

In Module 04 you ran someone else's Skill. Now you write one. Claude does almost all the mechanical work — the structure, the wording, the steps. And it's still only as good as the expertise you put in, because a Skill holds your conclusions, not the judgment that produced them.

1Draft the Skill

Open Claude with the skill-creator and describe the recap you run every week — in what order, and what you're looking for each time. Then ask it to build the Skill:

Help me turn my weekly lemonade-stand recap into a reusable Skill: the steps I follow, what goes in, what comes out.

In a minute you'll have a tidy Skill — named steps, an input list, a clean output format. It looks finished. It isn't tested yet.

2Test it where you know the answer

Hand it a week you've already reviewed in your own head — one where you already know what the right recap looks like:

Here's a real week I've already reviewed myself. Run the Skill on it and show the output. Then I'll tell you where it's wrong — and each correction is a piece of my judgment the Skill was missing. Fold those in and show me the diff.

The output will be close, but not right. Every place it's wrong is a place your judgment lived and the Skill didn't — so each correction you make is a piece of you the box left out.

The limit

What a skill can't hold

A Skill holds your conclusions, not the judgment behind them. Your price was right for your corner that summer — written down, it quietly becomes the price for everyone, everywhere. Watch what gets left out when you compress a method into steps. Some of what you know can be bottled. Some of it can't, no matter how carefully you try.

Checkpoint

Did your Skill pass the week you'd already judged yourself — and what did you have to add to make it pass?

Find the missing judgment

Put your output next to your corrected version and ask:

Compare your output to my corrected version. What piece of judgment did I apply that isn't written anywhere in the Skill? State it as a rule — and tell me if it even CAN be stated as a rule.

The corrections are the expertise. If a correction can't be written as a rule, that's the part of you that doesn't travel.

A Skill that runs your method — plus a clear-eyed list of what it still can't carry.

In the era of AI your hard-won expertise is worth more, not less — it's the scarce input a Skill can't generate on its own. But some of it won't fit in the box. Knowing which is which is its own kind of expertise.
