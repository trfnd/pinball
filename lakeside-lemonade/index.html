Welcome. Let's open a lemonade stand.

A hands-on workshop on working well with AI — no technical background needed.

Adapted from the Harvard Business School Executive Education course Lakeside Lemonade

What this is all aboutAI isn't magic, and it isn't useless. It's a new kind of colleague.

Most people try an AI tool a few times, get something bland or slightly wrong, and quietly decide it's either overhyped or not for them. That's a shame, because the gap between a disappointing answer and a genuinely useful one usually has very little to do with the technology. It has to do with how we work with it — what we tell it, what we ask for, what we check, and what we never hand over.

The good news: that's a human skill, not a technical one. If you've ever briefed a new colleague, reviewed someone's draft, or decided who on your team should handle what, you already have most of it. Today you'll practice turning that experience into a way of working with Claude.

Why a lemonade stand? Because nobody's career depends on it, so it's safe to experiment and get things wrong. And yet the decisions are real — who to sell to, where to stand, what to charge, how to keep the books — and the data behind them is real too: twelve interviews, a full cost sheet, a survey of every drink in town. Every habit you build on the stand travels straight back to your own work.

How the workshop worksOne module at a time, together

Do a module (15–25 min)→ Write your reflections→ Look up for the debrief→ Next module

Keep Claude open in another browser tab — you'll go back and forth between this page and Claude. When everyone has finished a module, your facilitator brings the room together to talk about what happened and what it means for real work. Then on to the next one.

Every module page has the same three parts:

Left · The setupThe situation, your part in it, the big idea, and a few words worth knowing.
Middle · Do itStep-by-step: try something in Claude, see what happens, understand why, try it again — with things to click, guess, sort and discover along the way.
Right · ReflectTwo or three short questions. Your answers save as you type and become your report.

Before you startA few things to know

First tries are supposed to be rough.Everyone's first answer from Claude is generic. Seeing why — and fixing it — is the lesson.
There are no wrong reflections.Honest beats polished. Short is fine. Nobody is grading you.
Bring your real work in.The stand is practice. Whenever a page asks "what about your work?", think of something real.
Keep confidential things out.Use the course files today. Don't paste anything sensitive into a tool your organization hasn't approved.
