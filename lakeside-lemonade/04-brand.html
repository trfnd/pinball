Don't know where to start?
Point Claude at the brand-book Skill (or install it), then give it your Part 1 decisions.

You don't know what belongs in a brand book — a palette? a tone? a tagline? The Skill does. It runs a short interview, asks about your corner and your customer, and hands back a name, colours, and a voice. The first pass usually looks slick and a little soulless.

1Run the Skill

Point Claude at the brand-book Skill and ask for the obvious thing:

Use the brand-book Skill to create a brand book for my lemonade stand.

The first result often reads like a generic kit: tasteful, plausible, and true of almost any lemonade stand.

2Make it yours

Feed it the specifics only you have:

This feels generic. Rebuild it around what's actually true for my stand: my corner is the waterfront path, my customers are joggers and weekend families, and my story is a small family business, not a national brand. Give it a voice that would sound wrong coming from any other stand — and tell me which choices you changed and why.

Now the brand book says something only your stand could say. The name, the colours, the voice all point back at the waterfront path and the family behind the table.

Where it comes from

A skill is one person's method, written down

That's how you got a brand book without knowing what goes in one — you ran someone else's method. Whether a Skill is any good depends on who wrote it and whether it was tested on cases where the right answer is already known. And when everyone installs the same kit, the work gets better and more alike: your competitors can run the exact same Skill. What you corrected or added is the only part nobody else has.

In a study of 293 fiction writers, AI-suggested ideas made stories better-rated but more similar to each other; a field experiment with 758 consultants found the same — better ideas, less variety. (Doshi & Hauser, Science Advances 2024; Dell'Acqua et al., Organization Science 2026.)

Checkpoint

Does your brand book now hold at least one thing that's true only for your stand — not for any lemonade stand?

Push past the kit

Add this and run it again:

Name three brand choices here that would be WRONG for a different lemonade stand two corners away. If you can't, the brand book is still generic — fix that.

The Skill gives you the scaffolding fast. The part that's worth anything is what you pushed back on.

A brand book with your fingerprints on it, not just the kit's.

A Skill gives you a method; you still supply the judgment. The scaffolding is commodity — your competitors can install it tomorrow. The edge is the correction only you could make.
