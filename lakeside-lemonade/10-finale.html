You've prompted, placed, priced, branded, launched, built, delegated, split, packaged, and verified — on a stand that sells cold lemonade on a hot day. None of that was really about lemonade. It was about the moves that matter when AI meets real work. So: your work. Where would you actually bring this in, and what would have to be true for you to trust it?

Answer the five parts of the brief on the right — short is fine, honest is the point. They map to the hardest questions the course raised: what's the real problem, where does the data live, where does a human have to stay in the loop, who owns it, and who could lose if it goes wrong. Together they're your deployment brief.

What you're really writing

Anyone can be excited about AI. The useful thing is a brief that names the problem worth solving, respects where the data really lives, keeps a human at the checkpoint that matters, puts a name on the owner, and is honest about who bears the risk. That's the difference between a demo and a deployment.
