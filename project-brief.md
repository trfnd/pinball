# Project brief

_Last updated: 2026-09-25_

## Confirmed facts

- Owner: Ibrahim Turfanda, physician and pinball collector.
- No machines ordered or installed for this venture. No venue deal signed.
- Staff at Westfield Collective seem open to *discussing* the concept. The GM is Angie, and her email address is on file.
- Early conversations with a few other local breweries. None has agreed to host machines.
- The Star Wars: Fall of the Empire machine is a **Premium** with topper and LE-level upgrades, not an LE (earlier notes were wrong). It has no bill acceptor.
- Transformers LE with topper and Dune are in the owner's collection.
- A one-page proposal and private meeting notes have been prepared.
- Research done: 38 Electric Bat Cast installments, three operator seminars, and the visible text of 253 topics in Pinside's operating/routing section. This informs the approach but does not prove local demand or economics.

## Owner preferences

- Self-service play, with occasional gatherings or friendly challenges.
- Welcoming to families, kids and teens, and casual players, while still appealing to enthusiasts.
- Payments independent of the venue's checkout, offering both cash and cashless.
- Four balls per game on harder titles, to improve the first-play experience.
- Do about half of ongoing repairs personally, plus routine installation.

## Modeling assumptions (not agreed, not measured)

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Revenue split | 70% Lucky Pigeon / 30% venue, after processing fees, refunds and chargebacks |
| Pricing | $1 per game or 3 for $2. Modeled at $1 per paid game, which is an upper bound; 3 for $2 averages about $0.67 per game |
| Maintenance | $200/month for four machines: $100 parts and consumables + $100 outside labor (2 h × $50/h) |
| Bill acceptors | $300 each where needed, plus accessories, tax and shipping |
| Owner time | About 2 visits/week, up to 8 h/week. Untested |
| Loss tolerance | Up to $5,000 total after a one-year exit. A working tolerance, not a budget or a guaranteed limit |

## Open questions

1. How much new cash does the opening lineup need, and what are the purchase prices versus realistic resale after 1, 2 and 3 years?
2. What terms will the venue actually offer: split, rent or minimum charges, electricity, insurance requirements, notice period? Who has final approval?
3. Traffic by day and hour, how long guests stay, age mix, and the hours minors are allowed.
4. Where the machines would go: floor space, power, delivery access, connectivity, spill and sun exposure.
5. Staff role in handling refunds, out-of-order signs and first response.
6. Payment equipment and provider, and how the venue verifies its share.
7. Insurance, business entity and any local amusement-device licensing.
8. How much wear on collection machines is acceptable.
9. Pilot length and pass/fail thresholds, to be written down before launch.
10. Maximum new-cash budget, set separately from the loss tolerance.
